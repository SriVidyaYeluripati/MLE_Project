# Pre-registration — confirmatory test of the `LQ_FIX_TRAPPED` coins effect

Written **before** any of the confirmatory runs were launched. This is the
confirmatory half of an exploratory/confirmatory split. `PREREG_trapped.md` is
the exploratory half; its runs are already complete and are **not** pooled with
these.

## What is being confirmed, and why it needs confirming

The exploratory run repaired `opp_trapped` (see `PREREG_trapped.md` for the bug)
and retrained 2 arms x 6 seeds with **kills** as the pre-registered primary.

| paired, n=6, exploratory | mean | better in | t |
|---|---|---|---|
| kills (was primary) | +0.031 | 4/6 | +1.10 |
| **coins** | **+0.328** | **5/6** | **+2.76** |
| score | +0.481 | 5/6 | +2.14 |
| margin | +0.611 | 5/6 | +2.47 |
| bombs | -2.52 | 3/6 | -1.09 |

The primary was a null. The coins row cannot be promoted, because
`PREREG_trapped.md` listed "switching to score or margin because kills says
nothing" as one of three things that would make that experiment worthless.

So it is treated as what it is: **a hypothesis generated by an exploratory
run**, tested here on fresh seeds with the metric fixed in advance.

## Hypothesis and metric — fixed now

> Retraining on the repaired `opp_trapped` raises coins per round.

**Primary metric: coins per round.** One-sided, because the direction was
predicted in advance by the exploratory half. Coins is also the least noisy unit
available (finding 20): coins replicate to +-0.05 against margin's +-0.10.

**Decision rule:** paired by seed, `t > 2.262` (one-sided, alpha = .05, df = 9).
Anything else is a null, and gets written up as one.

Secondary, reported with the primary and claimed only if the primary passes:
kills, score, margin, bombs.

## n is fixed at 10 per arm, now, before any data

Not 6. The exploratory estimate (d = 1.13) is itself the largest of several
metrics looked at in a noisy n=6 run, so it is very likely inflated — the
winner's curse. The honest design powers against **half** the observed effect:

| n per arm | power at +0.328 | power at +0.164 |
|---|---|---|
| 6 | 76% | 33% |
| 8 | 89% | 42% |
| **10** | **95%** | **50%** |
| 12 | 98% | 57% |

n = 10 is where the cost stops buying much. 20 trainings, roughly six hours on
two cores.

**The n is locked here so that it cannot be extended after looking.** Adding
seeds until a result appears is the same error as choosing a metric after
looking, and this project has already made that error three times (findings
15-17, 23). If the container is lost mid-run, the achieved n is reported with
its power recomputed — that missingness is caused by infrastructure and is
independent of the outcome, so it does not bias the test.

## Arms

Seeds **4001-4010**, fresh; the exploratory run used 3001-3006 and those are
not reused. Everything else identical to the exploratory arm: warm start from
`weights.npz`, `LQ_BOMB_COST=0 LQ_CRATE_VALUE=0.02`, 600 rounds on `classic`
against three `peaceful_agent`s, evaluated 300 rounds against three
`coin_collector_agent`s with learning off, one evaluation per weight vector.

| tag | switch |
|---|---|
| `cbase` | none |
| `cfix` | `LQ_FIX_TRAPPED=1` at train **and** eval |

## The mechanism, and the problem with it

The proposed mechanism is that a feature firing on 100% of covered opponents
made the agent chase traps that did not exist, and that a feature firing on the
genuine 5% frees those steps for collecting.

**This mechanism cannot act directly, and that is stated now rather than after
the result.** `OPP_IN_BLAST` and `OPP_TRAPPED` are assigned inside
`if is_bomb and ctx['bombs_left']` (features.py:481), so both are **zero on
every movement row**. They change the value of pressing BOMB and nothing else.
There is no direct gradient from either feature to a movement choice.

An indirect route exists: bombing differently changes where the agent stands,
and roughly 150 of its 368 steps are the post-bomb retreat, so bombing less
around opponents would free steps. **That route makes a prediction**, and it is
registered here as a secondary:

> if the mechanism is real, `cfix` should bomb **less** than `cbase`.

The exploratory data does not support it (bombs -2.52, t = -1.09, 3/6). If
coins rise again with bombs unmoved, the effect is real but unexplained, and it
must be reported that way.

## What would make this worthless

- Extending past n = 10, or stopping early, after looking at any result.
- Reporting kills, score or margin as the finding if coins says nothing.
- Pooling these seeds with the exploratory six.
- Reporting this as confirmation of the *bug* rather than of the *coins effect*.
  The bug is established independently, by code inspection and a 443/443
  behavioural match, and does not depend on this experiment either way.

## Outcome, appended after the run

Run 2026-09-07 in the project codespace, all 10 seeds, all 20 evaluations.

| paired, fix minus base, n=10 | mean | sd | better in | t | 95% CI |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **coins (PRIMARY)** | +0.160 | 0.748 | 6/10 | **+0.68** | [-0.375, +0.695] |
| kills | -0.020 | 0.087 | 5/10 | -0.73 | [-0.082, +0.042] |
| score | +0.056 | 1.004 | 4/10 | +0.18 | [-0.662, +0.774] |
| bombs | +1.73 | 6.45 | 7/10 | +0.85 | predicted *down* |
| margin | +0.127 | 1.208 | 4/10 | +0.33 | - |

**NULL on the pre-registered primary.** The exploratory +0.328 does not
replicate and is withdrawn as a candidate finding. The secondary mechanism
test also failed, and in the wrong direction: bombs went up, not down.

### Two errors in this document, recorded rather than quietly fixed

1. The decision rule above says `t > 2.262` and calls it one-sided. 2.262 is
   the **two-sided** .05 value at df=9; the one-sided value is **1.833**. The
   analysis script computed 1.833 correctly. Immaterial here (t = 0.68 fails
   both), but a pre-registration only checked when convenient is not one.

2. The power table above was built on the exploratory sd of 0.291. The true
   sd is **0.748**, so the promised "95% power at the observed effect" was
   really **36%**. Corrected:

   | effect | true power at n=10 |
   |---|---|
   | +0.164 | 16% |
   | +0.328 | 36% |
   | +0.500 | 62% |
   | +0.700 | 86% |

   So this is **not** a tight bounded null. It rules out a gain above ~+0.7
   coins and cannot exclude a real +0.3. Detecting +0.3 at 80% power would
   need n = 40 per arm - 80 trainings.

### The lesson

The exploratory run over-stated the effect **and** under-stated its spread,
and both are one selection artefact: a metric is picked out of five precisely
on the run where its numerator ran high and its denominator ran low.

> Never power a confirmatory study on the variance estimate from the
> exploratory run that motivated it. Take the spread from a control condition
> measured for its own sake, and treat the exploratory effect as an upper
> bound.

### What is unaffected

The bug itself. It follows from three lines of code, was confirmed by
identical firing on 443 of 443 steps, and is visible in four weights sitting
at exactly +0.0427. Whether repairing it pays was the question here; whether
it is broken was never in doubt.
